Why Networthy

Things most finance apps don't do

Not a longer feature list — a few specific things that actually require different math, not just a prettier chart.

Private investments tracked with a real return, not just a balance

Own a stake in a private business, an angel investment, or a fund with no live price feed? Log a value periodically and Networthy computes XIRR and P&L for it, the same as a brokerage holding. It even knows the difference between a business paying you profit and you actually withdrawing your investment — most apps either ignore private holdings entirely or track a bare balance with no return at all.

True multi-broker average cost, not side-by-side accounts

Buy the same stock across three brokers and Networthy merges every lot into one real average-cost position — while still letting you drill into any single broker's own lots. Most trackers just list your accounts next to each other; this actually does the cost-basis math.

Time-weighted and money-weighted return, side by side

One chart shows your pure market performance (TWR) next to your actual annualized return given when you added or withdrew money (MWR/XIRR) — with a plain-language explainer built in. Most retail apps blend these into a single "return %" that answers neither question precisely.

Dividend income, net of the tax you actually paid

Per-country withholding-tax rates (Singapore, US, Japan, and more), editable if a rate changes or a new market isn't covered yet. Consumer apps typically show gross dividends and stop there.

Group your portfolio however you actually think about it

Define your own groupings — by country, by sector, by conviction level, whatever — instead of being stuck with one fixed taxonomy someone else chose for you.

No bank-credential aggregator, no company server

Your data lives on your device. No Plaid-style account linking, no financial data sitting on a server we operate. If you want a backup, it goes to your own Google Drive — we never see it.

Switch from your old tracker in minutes, not by re-typing years of history

Bulk-import transactions straight from Excel, with fuzzy broker and account-name matching, automatic date-format detection, and clear per-row error reporting if something doesn't match. Most trackers assume you're starting from zero.

Private Investments

The holdings no other tracker knows what to do with

A stake in a private business, an angel investment, a fund with no ticker — Networthy tracks these with a real return, not just a static balance.

Log a value, get a real return — profit handled correctly

No live price feed for a private holding, so you check in periodically and log what it's worth — Networthy computes XIRR and P&L the same way it does for a brokerage position, rolling the value forward for any cash that's moved since your last check-in. It also knows the difference between a business paying you profit and you withdrawing your investment: a running pool of what's actually been earned nets against withdrawals first, so a real payout shows up as profit, not a capital pull-out.
