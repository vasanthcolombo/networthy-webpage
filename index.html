Why Networthy

Things most finance apps don't do

Not a longer feature list — a few specific things that actually require different math, not just a prettier chart.

True multi-broker average cost, not side-by-side accounts

Buy the same stock across three brokers and Networthy merges every lot into one real average-cost position — while still letting you drill into any single broker's own lots. Most trackers just list your accounts next to each other; this actually does the cost-basis math.

Time-weighted and money-weighted return, side by side

One chart shows your pure market performance (TWR) next to your actual annualized return given when you added or withdrew money (MWR/XIRR) — with a plain-language explainer built in. Most retail apps blend these into a single "return %" that answers neither question precisely.

Dividend income, net of the tax you actually paid

Per-country withholding-tax rates (Singapore, US, Japan, and more), editable if a rate changes or a new market isn't covered yet. Consumer apps typically show gross dividends and stop there.

Group your portfolio however you actually think about it

Define your own groupings — by country, by sector, by conviction level, whatever — instead of being stuck with one fixed taxonomy someone else chose for you.

No bank-credential aggregator, no company server

Your data lives on your device. No Plaid-style account linking, no financial data sitting on a server we operate. If you want a backup, it goes to your own Google Drive — we never see it.
